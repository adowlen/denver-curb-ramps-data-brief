## LinkedIn draft (company page, for a future Friday synthesis)

563 vs 663.

Denver's highest pedestrian-demand zones hold 563.3 curb ramps per
square mile. Its medium-demand zones hold 663.1. That is the
area-weighted count across all 479 of the city's Pedestrian Demand
Index zones and 61,983 ramps placed inside them - and 27 of the 191
high-demand zones sit below 300 ramps per square mile.

The demand side of the index checks out against outcomes:
pedestrian crashes are 3.1% of crashes in high-demand zones against
2.0% in low-demand zones, and 42.2 pedestrian crashes per square
mile against 7.7. One limit rides with every number here: a ramp
point is a location seen from aerial imagery, not an ADA-compliance
finding, and land area is not an intersection count. The brief
prints both the area-weighted and median-zone figures, and the
strongest alternative explanation, with the data.

Full brief, data, and methods:
