# Hero brief - Denver curb ramps brief (2026-10-28)

Status: hero.png is NOT in this package. Image generation is handled

## Headline figure to composite (exact text)

Main figure: "563 vs 663"
Sub-line: "Curb ramps per square mile: Denver's highest pedestrian-demand zones (563.3) hold fewer than its medium-demand zones (663.1)"
Source line: "Machine Readable - Denver curb ramp layer and Pedestrian Demand Index, pulled Oct 28, 2026 - 61,983 ramps in 479 zones"

## Scene description (story-driven, not decorative)

A nighttime-to-dusk street corner scene in a dense city neighborhood,
seen from a slightly elevated angle: a pedestrian with a cane and a
person using a wheelchair wait at a corner where the curb ramp is
missing on their side of the street - the curb runs unbroken - while
across the street, in warm amber light, a corner with a clear ramp
and a steady stream of people walking shows the contrast. The near
corner sits in cooler teal shadow, the far corner in amber. The
visual argument is the brief's: where walking demand is highest, the
corner infrastructure is not automatically densest. Keep figures
dignified and generic, no faces in close-up, no text baked into the
art itself.

## Composition requirements

- Brand palette: dark charcoal base, amber and teal accents.
- Leave a clean, dark, low-detail field across the top third (or a
  gradient scrim over it) for the headline figure; headline text must
  be fully legible, never washed out over busy art.
- Composite the headline figure and source line from the exact text
  above; do not paraphrase the numbers.
- The illustration must not imply an ADA-compliance finding about any
  real corner: it is an illustration of the density finding, and the
  brief's caveat (a ramp point is a location, not a compliance
  finding) applies to the graphic package as a whole.
